WHO growth guide

What does your baby’s growth percentile actually mean?

A percentile is a comparison with a reference population, not a grade or a target. Learn how to read it and why the pattern and clinical context matter more than a single number.

A plain-language percentile example

If a baby is at the 49th percentile for weight-for-age, about 49 out of 100 children of the same age and sex in the reference population weigh less, and about 51 weigh more.

That does not mean the baby is 49 percent healthy, 49 percent grown, or expected to move toward the 100th percentile. Children have different bodies and growth patterns. The 10th and 90th percentiles are not grades either.

Weight, height and head circumference answer different questions

  • Weight-for-age places a measured weight in age and sex reference context.
  • Length or height-for-age tracks another dimension of growth over time.
  • Head circumference-for-age preserves a separate measurement commonly collected in early-childhood care.

Looking at these together is more informative than isolating one reading. Feeding, health, prematurity, genetics, measurement technique and clinical examination can all affect interpretation.

The pattern matters more than chasing a line

A single measurement can vary with scale, clothing, timing or technique. A series of carefully recorded measurements helps a professional understand the pattern. Crossing a percentile line is a reason for a qualified review, not something an app should diagnose.

The WHO Child Growth Standards describe the reference charts and methods. Your country or clinician may use another reference, particularly as children grow older or when prematurity needs to be considered.

Ask a professional when you are concerned

Do not wait for an app to label a measurement. Contact your pediatrician or child health service about poor feeding, hydration concerns, persistent changes or anything else that worries you.

Common questions

Is a higher baby percentile better?
No. A percentile is not a grade. Health professionals interpret the measurement alongside growth pattern, examination and history.
Should every baby be near the 50th percentile?
No. Healthy children sit at many different percentiles. The 50th is the middle of a reference population, not a universal target.
Does Feed Flow diagnose growth problems?
No. It records measurements and shows WHO reference context. A qualified professional must assess growth and health.
